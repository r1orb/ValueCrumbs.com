The crumbs, explained

The best investors leave a trail. Every quarter they file their portfolios with the SEC, company insiders report every share they buy, and anyone crossing 5% of a company has to say so. ValueCrumbs follows that trail for you.

What we track

A bot reads the SEC's EDGAR database every few minutes and turns new filings into short, readable "crumbs". Each one links to the original document, so you can always check the source.

Insider buys

Directors and officers buying their own company's stock with their own money, in the open market (Form 4, code P). Planned trades under 10b5-1 plans and sales are left out. Insiders must report within two business days.

Cluster buys

Several insiders of the same company buying within a few days of each other: one of the patterns investors watch most closely.

13F changes

New, increased, reduced and closed positions in the quarterly portfolios of the investors we follow. 13Fs are filed up to 45 days after quarter end and only show long positions in US-listed stocks.

5%+ stakes

Schedule 13D (activist) and 13G (passive) filings by those investors when they own more than 5% of a company.

What this is not

  • Not investment advice. We report what public filings say. We do not tell anyone to buy or sell anything, and nothing here takes your situation into account.
  • Not real time. Filings arrive days, sometimes weeks, after the trades they describe. By the time you read a crumb, the investor may already have changed their mind.
  • Not infallible. Filings can be amended, and turning thousands of documents into crumbs can go wrong. Always check the linked filing before relying on a crumb.

Where the data comes from

All crumbs come from public filings in the SEC EDGAR database. Tickers for 13F positions are matched from their CUSIP codes through OpenFIGI. ValueCrumbs is independent and not affiliated with the SEC or with any of the investors mentioned.
